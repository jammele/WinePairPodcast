If Cabernet Sauvignon is your wine, you already know what you want: tannin you can feel, dark fruit that fills the whole mouth, and a finish that sticks around long after the glass is empty. Cab is the most planted red grape in the world for a reason. It delivers the kind of wine that makes a great steak better and a mediocre one irrelevant.

It also has a style family. There are wines that give you pieces of that experience: the dark fruit without the price, the structure with a different country's personality underneath, the boldness taken in a different direction. Some of these are obvious. Some are worth knowing.

Here are six wines to try if Cabernet Sauvignon is already in your rotation.

---

## Cabernet Sauvignon

[INSERT CARD: Cabernet Sauvignon Anchor]

Cabernet Sauvignon is the benchmark bold red, and it knows it. Grown almost everywhere wine is made but at its best in Bordeaux, Napa Valley, and Chile, Cab is defined by its tannin structure: firm, gripping, and built to last. The flavor profile runs toward blackberry and cassis, with cedar, pencil shavings, and a hint of espresso from the barrel. Young Cab can be aggressive. Given time or paired with fat and protein, it opens into something genuinely excellent.

Napa Valley Cab is the gold standard for most American drinkers: riper, more fruit-forward, and more polished than its French counterpart. Chilean Cab gives you much of the same at half the price. Bordeaux adds more earthiness and complexity. All of them share the structural backbone that makes the grape what it is.

## Merlot

[INSERT CARD: Merlot]

**What you'll recognize from Cabernet Sauvignon:** Dark fruit in the same family: plum, black cherry, a little chocolate. Similar body and some oak character. You're still in Bordeaux country. The two grapes are closely related and regularly blended together.

**What's different:** Merlot has softer, more velvety tannins. Less grip, more round and plush. Where Cab makes you work a little, Merlot just drinks. The flavor profile goes toward plum and fig where Cab runs toward cassis and blackberry. It is also more immediately drinkable, usually without needing the decanter or the steak to open it up.

**Try this if:** Cabernet is a bit much sometimes. Merlot is the gentler version of the same wine-drinking instinct. Right Bank Bordeaux (Saint-Emilion, Pomerol) is where Merlot gets serious; California Merlot is where it gets approachable and easy.

## Malbec

[INSERT CARD: Malbec]

**What you'll recognize from Cabernet Sauvignon:** Bold dark fruit, real body, and the kind of warmth that belongs with red meat. Both are wines built for a steak dinner.

**What's different:** Malbec is smoother and less tannic. The fruit goes toward blueberry and chocolate rather than blackberry and cedar. Less structured, more immediately drinkable. Malbec from Argentina doesn't need a big steak or two hours of decanting to show you what it has. It is also usually less expensive.

**Try this if:** You want the dark fruit and body of Cab without the grip. Malbec is the most approachable entry in this category and one of the better everyday values in red wine.

## Syrah / Shiraz

[INSERT CARD: Syrah/Shiraz]

**What you'll recognize from Cabernet Sauvignon:** Dark fruit, serious tannin, real body. Both are wines that hold their own next to food. You'll find blackberry, a hint of smoke, and a long finish in the same territory as a good Cab.

**What's different:** Syrah goes savory where Cab goes fruit-and-cedar. Black pepper, smoked meat, sometimes olive or bacon fat. Northern Rhone Syrah from France is structured and violet-scented, built to age. Australian Shiraz is the louder version: more fruit, more alcohol, more warmth. Same grape, very different personality depending on where you're buying.

**Try this if:** You want Cab's structure and dark fruit but want something that makes you think. Syrah is the natural next step for any Cab drinker who is ready to explore.

## Rioja (Tempranillo)

[INSERT CARD: Rioja]

**What you'll recognize from Cabernet Sauvignon:** Real structure and oak presence. Rioja Reserva is a wine with backbone: tannin you can feel, a long finish, and the unmistakable character of time in barrel. Cab drinkers recognize the seriousness immediately. (The episode we did on Rioja is literally subtitled "for Cab Sauv lovers.")

**What's different:** The fruit profile is completely different. Rioja runs toward cherry, tobacco, and vanilla rather than blackberry and cedar. It is earthier and more old-world. Tempranillo (the main grape in Rioja) also has higher acidity than Cab, which gives it more food-friendliness and a brighter finish.

**Try this if:** You want Cab's structure at better prices. Rioja Reserva under $20 is one of the best deals in wine. And if you have never tried Rioja with lamb, fix that immediately.

## Barolo

[INSERT CARD: Barolo]

**What you'll recognize from Cabernet Sauvignon:** The tannin. Barolo is made from Nebbiolo in Piedmont, which carries some of the most powerful tannins of any red grape. Long finish. A wine that needs food and rewards patience. Cab lovers recognize the weight immediately even though the flavor is completely different.

**What's different:** Barolo is more complex and more demanding. The fruit character goes toward dried cherry and rose rather than blackberry and cassis. There is truffle, tar, and leather underneath. Higher acidity. It is the Italian equivalent of serious Bordeaux: built to age, built to pair, worth the effort. Entry-level Barolo from Costco or Trader Joe's (yes, really) is a good place to start.

**Try this if:** You already drink Cab regularly and want to know what Italian fine wine tastes like. Barolo is where Cab lovers land when they are ready for more complexity.

## Zinfandel

[INSERT CARD: Zinfandel]

**What you'll recognize from Cabernet Sauvignon:** The weight. Both are big, full-bodied wines. Both have dark fruit. Both stand up to red meat without any problem.

**What's different:** Zinfandel is jammier and more fruit-driven. Blackberry jam rather than fresh blackberry. Higher alcohol, almost always. Less structured tannin and more spice running through the wine: cinnamon, black pepper, mocha. California through and through.

**Try this if:** You want the same pairing profile (steak, BBQ, burgers) but something looser and more purely fun. Zinfandel is the American alternative to Cab when you want bold without the Bordeaux structure.

## Food pairings that work across the group

Cabernet Sauvignon's high tannin is the key to its food-pairing logic. Tannin binds to protein and fat, which is why Cab with a ribeye is one of the classic pairings in wine: the tannin grips the fat, the fat softens the tannin, and both get better. That logic carries across every wine on this list.

Merlot and Malbec handle lighter red meat a little better. Rioja adds versatility with lamb and chorizo. Barolo is the best of the group with truffle and mushroom-heavy dishes. Zinfandel and Syrah are the best for BBQ.

Pairings that work for all six:

- Ribeye or New York strip steak, grilled or roasted
- Burgers (the juicier the better)
- Lamb chops, rack of lamb, or leg of lamb
- Brisket, short rib, or slow-braised beef
- Hard aged cheese: aged cheddar, manchego, aged gouda, pecorino
- Mushroom dishes: risotto, pasta with mushroom ragu, grain bowls

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. What wine is similar to Cabernet Sauvignon but easier to drink?**
A. Merlot. It's in the same Bordeaux family, uses the same dark fruit vocabulary (plum, black cherry, chocolate), and has similar body. But the tannins are softer and more round. Where Cab needs food or time to open up, Merlot just drinks. If you like Cab but find yourself always reaching for the decanter, try a bottle of Right Bank Bordeaux or a good California Merlot first.

**Q. Is Malbec similar to Cabernet Sauvignon?**
A. Close, but not quite the same. Both are bold, dark-fruited reds with real body. But Malbec is smoother and less tannic: it doesn't have the same grip. The fruit goes toward blueberry and chocolate rather than Cab's blackberry and cedar. If Cab feels like it requires effort (a steak, a decanter, time), Malbec is the version that doesn't. Also: Malbec is technically one of Bordeaux's six permitted blending varieties, so they share the same old address.

**Q. What's the difference between Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah?**
A. Cab is fruitier and more structured; Syrah is spicier and more savory. Both are bold reds with dark fruit and real tannin, but Syrah adds black pepper, smoked meat, and sometimes olive to the profile. Cab's signature flavors are blackberry, cedar, and espresso. They pair similarly with food (both love steak and grilled meat), but Syrah is a noticeably different experience if you're paying attention.

**Q. What should I try after Cabernet Sauvignon?**
A. Barolo. If Cab is already in your rotation and you want to know what great wine can do, Nebbiolo from Piedmont is the destination. Same weight and tannin power, but completely different flavor: dried cherry, rose, truffle, tar. More complex, more demanding, and worth the effort. Start with an entry-level Barolo or a good Barbaresco (same grape, more approachable style) and see if it grabs you.

**Q. Are there good Cabernet Sauvignon alternatives under $20?**
A. Yes. Rioja Reserva is the best value: real structure, real oak aging, real wine for $12-20. Chilean Cabernet Sauvignon is another strong option in the same range. If you want something even easier to drink, Malbec from Mendoza gives you similar dark fruit and body with a friendlier tannin structure, often for $15-20.
