### Extended Discovery Research
Triggered. Trigger reasons: (1) a buying/value/provenance question is plausible for this episode (price-rule exception, producer identity); (2) Henners' producer identity is a "notable/surprising name" candidate family per §3.

Findings: Henners was founded in 2007 by ex-Formula One engineer Lawrence Warr on a 7-acre East Sussex vineyard, named for his French ancestor Henners Dubois who fled Paris during the French Revolution (episode evidence only; not independently searched, see Rejection Notes for why this candidate was not advanced). Separately, extended research into the two named historical claims that anchor the episode's hook:
- Christopher Merret's 1662 paper: confirmed via GuildSomm (Courtney Schiessl) as "Some Observations Concerning the Ordering of Wines," read to the Royal Society December 17, 1662, describing British "wine coopers" making wines "brisk and sparkling" via sugar addition. GlassofBubbly separately confirms the Merret/1662 attribution but flags the "England invented sparkling wine" framing as "highly contested and disputed" (contested as to credit/priority, not as to the 1662 date itself).
- Nyetimber's 1988 founding: confirmed via Wikipedia (cross-checked against iandagatawine.com) — American couple Stuart and Sandy Moss bought the estate in 1986, planted Chardonnay/Pinot Noir/Pinot Meunier in 1988 (England's first plantings made exclusively for sparkling wine), first vintage Blanc de Blancs 1992 released 1996, winning gold at the International Wine and Spirit Competition and the Best English Wine trophy. Wikipedia's account does **not** confirm this was a head-to-head win against Champagne entries specifically, which narrows the episode's "winning gold medals against Champagne" framing (see Corroboration Table).
- Domestic sales growth and vineyard count: confirmed via WineGB (winegb.co.uk, July 2024) and JM Finn — sparkling wine sales rose 187% since 2018, from 2.2 million to 6.2 million bottles (2023 figure), and UK vineyards surpassed 1,000 in 2023 (1,030 per WineGB's report).
- 2023 harvest volume: confirmed via gov.uk — "2023 is Great Britain's largest-ever grape harvest, expected to produce an estimated 20-22m bottles."
- Global-scale comparison (England's 6.2M bottles vs. Champagne 266M / Prosecco 667M / Cava 190M, as stated in the episode): **not corroborated**. Official CIVC (Champagne trade body) shipment data for 2023 is 299 million bottles, not 266 million — a direct numeric conflict with the episode's figure, and no source was found supporting the specific 667M Prosecco / 190M Cava figures either. See Corroboration Table; the specific comparison numbers are dropped from all published content, replaced with the safely-supported qualitative point that England's volume is a small fraction of Champagne's alone.
- Climate change temperature shift: Wine Society confirms "average growing-season temperatures in southern England have risen two degrees since the 1970s" and that this now allows ripening "Champagne varieties." The episode's specific "~13°C to ~14.5°C" framing was not independently confirmed at a primary source in the time available for this run (only surfaced via an aggregated search-engine summary, which is Observed evidence, not a fetched citable source) — this specific figure is not used in published content; the corroborated 2°C-since-1970s framing is used instead where climate change appears (Bluesky post only; no Key Question was built primarily around this angle).
