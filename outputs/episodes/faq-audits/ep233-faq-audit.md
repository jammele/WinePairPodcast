# Ep233 FAQ Audit — Aglianico Rosato

### Episode Opportunity Frame
Episode type: standard two-wine review, drawing on both the "standard two-wine review" and "grape explainer" candidate-prompt families (`docs/faq-intent-model.md` §3), since the episode is structured as a head-to-head comparison but spends most of its runtime educating listeners on Aglianico rosato as a style: what it is, where it's regulated, and why a famously bold, high-tannin red grape makes a rosé worth taking seriously. The dominant listener opportunity is the episode's own repeated thesis/PSA: if you like a bold red wine (or its grape), try the rosé made from that same grape, tested here with two very different Aglianico-based Italian rosati that landed at different rating levels (San Salvatore Vetere 7/8, Feudi di San Gregorio San Greg 5/6). Secondary opportunities: rosé misconceptions the hosts address on air (blend myth, saignée vs. intentional production), and the DOCG/DOC regulatory detail (85% and 70% Aglianico minimums) that is unusually specific for this format.

### Episode-Evidence Ledger
- E1: Episode hook — Joe and Carmela previously dismissed rosé as unserious, now say they were wrong, testing their own PSA with two Aglianico-based Italian rosati.
- E2: Repeated on-air PSA/thesis — if you like a red wine (or its grape), try the rosé made from that same grape; presented as a recommendation, not a guarantee.
- E3: Wine 1 (San Salvatore Vetere Rosato, $26.97, 100% Aglianico, 11.5% ABV): Joe 7/10, Carmela 8/10; color very light pink/peach/blush; nose rose, cherry, peach, strawberry; palate strawberry/cherry, orange creamsicle, lemon sorbet, strawberries and cream, light, crisp; pairings versatile (fish, fried foods, spicy foods, brunch dishes).
- E4: Wine 2 (Feudi di San Gregorio San Greg Rosato, $25.97, 100% Aglianico, 12.5% ABV): Joe 5/10, Carmela 6/10; color pink/coral/artificial-looking; nose yeasty, white grape, citrus, grapefruit; palate grapefruit, "tastes like a Paloma cocktail," a little harsh, tea, very acidic, lime-juice finish; pairings white pizza, bready foods, focaccia, calzone; host note "it's a little harsh."
- E5: Both hosts chose the San Salvatore Vetere Rosato to finish the night.
- E6: Aglianico's naturally high tannin means even brief skin contact produces a rosé that's brighter in color and firmer in structure than typical rosé; tends to be dry, savory, complex despite pale color.
- E7: Aglianico del Taburno Rosato DOCG requires at least 85% Aglianico (Sannio Beneventano, 200-600m altitude vineyards).
- E8: Irpinia Rosato DOC (designated 2005) requires a minimum of 70% Aglianico.
- E9: In Basilicata, Aglianico rosé falls under the looser IGT designation; no required percentage, though many producers use 100% Aglianico, some aged in French oak.
- E10: Aglianico is grown across southern Italy, especially Campania (Naples, Taurasi) and Basilicata (Matera, Melfi, Mount Vulture).
- E11: A Wine Advocate reviewer named Aglianico one of the top five Italian grapes for rosé, alongside Sangiovese and Nerello Mascalese.
- E12: Rosé misconception addressed on air: it is not a blend of red and white wine; color comes from skin contact; some rosé is made via saignée as a red-wine byproduct, but most quality rosé today, including this episode's wines, is made intentionally as its own wine.
- E13: "Rosato" is simply the Italian word for rosé.
- E14: Feudi di San Gregorio's grapes are hand-picked on the highest hills of Irpinia (up to 700 meters), gently pressed, macerated on the skins for less than 2 hours, fermented/aged 4 months in stainless steel, aiming for an aperitif-style wine.
- E15: San Salvatore's Vetere Rosato uses 6 hours of cryomaceration, soft pressing with brief skin contact, stainless-steel fermentation, and 6 months lees aging in stainless steel.
- E16: The episode's own extended discussion of Paestum (where the San Salvatore wine's Paestum IGT designation is named) and the hosts' recollection of visiting it: Joe and Carmela discuss Paestum at length as "one of the most amazing places we've ever been," describing it as having Greek temples that were "part of Magna Grecia." This is Episode evidence about Paestum and Magna Graecia as a place the hosts discussed on air — it does not itself establish, and is not used to establish, that the San Salvatore wine's Paestum IGT designation carries any specific temple/UNESCO fact; Bluesky Post 10 states only the independently source-verified Paestum/Magna Graecia/temple/UNESCO facts (see Provisional Claim / Corroboration Table), not a claim about the wine's designation.
