And now it is time to head over to our news desk so that we can cover our wine in the news this week segment.

This week our wine in the news comes to us from Anna Sulkin Stern writing for Wealth Management. And the article is called "Estate Dispute Sees Potentially Largest Sanctions Imposed For AI-Related Misconduct."

Right away, this feels like a wild story, and the short version is this: four siblings got into a fight over who should own the family winery after their mother died, and one of them ended up in court with a lawyer who submitted fifteen fake AI-generated court cases to the judge. And then tried to cover it up.

Now let me back up, because there are some details here that are too good to skip.

The family is the Wisnovskys, and they own Valley View Winery in Jacksonville, Oregon. There are four kids: Robert, Joanne, Mark, and Michael. Mark and Michael are the younger brothers, and for years they were the ones actually running the winery. Back in 2015, their mother transferred vineyard control to Mark and Michael -- the two who were operating the business. That did not sit well with Joanne, the second oldest. She filed a lawsuit against her brothers accusing them of elder abuse and wrongful enrichment, and she was seeking twelve million dollars.

Now. Every lawsuit needs a lawyer. And here is where it gets interesting.

The lawyer Joanne ended up with was a man named Stephen Brigandi. Stephen Brigandi is a San Diego attorney. His previous career highlight was serving as a corporate lawyer for Jack in the Box. He agreed to take Joanne's case for free. The reason he agreed to work for free: his son is dating Joanne's daughter.

But wait, it gets more interesting.

Brigandi submitted legal briefs to the judge that contained fifteen fabricated case citations and eight made-up legal references. Not one. Fifteen. Some of the cases he cited did not exist anywhere. Others were real cases, but the quotations he pulled from them were completely invented.

But wait, it gets more interesting.

When the opposing attorneys flagged the fake citations, Brigandi did not come clean. He deleted them from the briefs and quietly refiled the documents -- without disclosing what he had done. The judge, Mark D. Clarke, caught on anyway. Judge Clarke dismissed Joanne's claims entirely, with prejudice -- meaning she cannot bring them again. And he imposed over one hundred and ten thousand dollars in sanctions between Brigandi and his local co-counsel. This has been called the largest penalty ever handed down in the United States for AI misconduct in a legal filing.

Joanne is planning to appeal.

Carmela, I have to ask you this. If we ever needed a lawyer in a hurry, and the only available option was a guy who used to work for Jack in the Box and is offering to do it for free -- do we take that deal?
