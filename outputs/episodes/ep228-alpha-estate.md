### FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
*(Place at bottom of show notes)*

**Q. What does Xinomavro taste like?**
A. Xinomavro is a high-acid, high-tannin Greek red, its name literally translates to "sour-black." Young Xinomavro shows red berries, black cherry, and savory notes like sun-dried tomato and olive. In Amyndeon specifically, high altitude and mineral-rich, sandy-over-limestone soil give the wine real finesse and balance alongside that structure.

**Q. What's the difference between PDO Amyndeon and PGI Florina?**
A. PDO Amyndeon is the stricter label: every grape must come from that specific high-altitude Amyndeon plateau, historically tied to 100% Xinomavro, covering dry red, rosé, and sparkling wines. PGI Florina is broader, requiring only 85% of grapes from the wider Florina prefecture (including Amyndeon) and allowing more grape varieties, similar in concept to Italy's DOCG system.

**Q. Why does high altitude matter for wine quality in Amyndeon?**
A. Amyndeon sits on a high plateau ringed by three mountains, with lakes nearby that moderate summer heat and winter cold. That altitude protects the vines from weather extremes, and combined with sandy, limestone-based soil left behind by an ancient lake, it gives Xinomavro grapes crisp acidity, minerality, and real structural finesse.

**Q. Is Xinomavro similar to Nebbiolo or Pinot Noir?**
A. Stylistically, yes. Winemaker Angelos Iatridis describes Xinomavro as landing somewhere between Nebbiolo and Pinot Noir, sharing Nebbiolo's high acid and firm tannin with Pinot Noir's lighter color and red-fruit lift. The comparison is about character, not genetics. Xinomavro is its own indigenous Greek grape with a flavor arc all its own.

**Q. What food pairs well with Xinomavro?**
A. Xinomavro's firm tannin and bright acidity make it a natural match for rich, savory food: roasted lamb, grilled meats, sausages, rabbit stew, peppers cooked in olive oil, or full-bodied cheeses. Winemaker Angelos Iatridis pointed to old-vine Xinomavro Reserve, from vines planted in 1919, as the wine he'd bring to a big family Sunday dinner of pasta and meat.

**Q. Who is Angelos Iatridis and why does his perspective matter for Greek wine?**
A. Angelos Iatridis is the co-founder and winemaker of Alpha Estate in Amyndeon, Greece, and a chemist-oenologist trained at Bordeaux University. He has spent decades turning a small, historically overlooked high-altitude plateau into one of Greece's most respected Xinomavro producers, now exporting to more than 50 countries.

**Q. What is Angelos Iatridis's approach to winemaking at Alpha Estate?**
A. Angelos Iatridis treats modern winery technology as what he calls "a precision tool, not a stylistic filter." Tools like optical sorting and gravity movement exist to protect what the vineyard already created, not to modernize the wine's style. His stated philosophy is high technology in the cellar paired with deep humility toward the vineyard.
