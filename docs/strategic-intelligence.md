## Entry 001 — Wine predictability research
**Date:** 2026-05-29
**Source:** Wine Market Council / Quini study, reported in Press Democrat (May 29, 2026)
**Finding:** Only 11% of consumers can predict wine flavor before purchase, compared to ~50% for beer and spirits. About 23% have bought a wine expecting flavors they ended up disliking. Wine-hesitant consumers average fewer than 10 wines tried across an entire decade. Researchers prescribe: describe wine in terms of sweetness, acidity, and mouthfeel; group wines by flavor profile; eliminate jargon like "terroir."
**Connects to:**
- The show's honest tasting notes and plain-language descriptions — these are a direct response to the predictability gap, whether or not it was named that way
- The "wines similar to X" spoke pages — grouping by flavor profile is exactly what researchers recommend as the solution; spoke pages are a structural implementation of this
- The Winedr app — flavor-profile grouping and the personality quiz are the app-layer version of the researcher prescription
- Anti-jargon voice — jargon is not just snobbery; it actively prevents consumers from predicting what they're buying
- The QPR / honest ratings framework — credible predictions require a trusted, independent source
**Open questions:**
- Do our blog post tasting notes currently lead with sweetness, acidity, and mouthfeel, or do they bury those dimensions under flavor notes?
- Are we explicitly addressing the hesitant "I've been burned before" consumer anywhere in the intro copy of blog posts or show notes?
- Does the similarity hub page brief make the predictability problem explicit as the reason the page exists?

## Entry 002 — Gen Z anti-condescension signal
**Date:** 2026-05-18
**Source:** "Gen Z to Wine: Please Stop Condescending" — Everyday Drinking (May 2026)
**Finding:** Gen Z is actively rejecting wine's condescension culture. They want honest approachability, not prestige gatekeeping. This is a cultural signal, not just a demographic data point — it suggests a generational shift in what wine communication should sound like.
**Connects to:**
- Brand voice — the show's anti-snob, husband-and-wife format is structurally aligned with what Gen Z says it wants from wine content
- Potential new listener segment — if Gen Z is actively looking for non-condescending wine content, the show may be better positioned for this audience than it currently knows
- SEO and content framing — "no judgment" and "you don't have to know wine to enjoy wine" are implicit in everything we write; could be made more explicit in pillar pages and the About section
**Open questions:**
- What share of current listeners are under 35? Would be useful to know whether this is already an audience or an opportunity.
- Does the show's Apple Podcasts description and Spotify bio reflect the anti-condescension angle prominently, or does it lead with wine education (which can read as gatekeeping)?
- When the Gen Z cohort searches for wine content, what queries do they use? Likely different from the typical "is X wine good" pattern.

## Entry 003 — Show description price-range drift
**Date:** 2026-06-14
**Source:** ChatGPT positioning/directory audit, cross-checked against the live Buzzsprout RSS description and recent episode pricing
**Finding:** The official show description and `docs/soul-document.md` both stated "under $25" as a near-absolute price ceiling ("wines under $25 each... easy to find"). But recent regular review episodes have already exceeded it: Ep217 Frappato was $27.97, Ep221 Lebanese wines were $25.97. "Under $25" is drifting from accurate to aspirational as the show covers more imported and unusual wines.
**Connects to:**
- `docs/soul-document.md` Accessibility Focus and "What We DON'T Cover" sections — updated 2026-06-14 from "under $25" to "usually under $25 / occasional bottles run a bit higher"
- New canonical positioning statement (`docs/soul-document.md` "Positioning Statement (canonical)") — adopts "usually for less than $25 a bottle" as the proof statement, the more honest framing
- Buzzsprout RSS description rewrite (`outputs/positioning-rollout.md`) — corrected from "under $25 each" to "usually under $25"
**Open questions:**
- Should there be an explicit soft ceiling stated anywhere (e.g., "rarely above $30"), or is "usually under $25" sufficient?
- Track future episode prices. If regular review episodes start regularly landing in the high $20s/low $30s, the soul doc accessibility framing may need a bigger rework, not just wording.
